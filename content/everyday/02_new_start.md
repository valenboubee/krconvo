## A. Context

Two people who are dating are texting in the evening. One of them starts a new
job next week and is a little nervous about it. Notice how the other person
**doesn't tell her what to do** — instead he **normalizes** the feeling,
**reframes** it in a kinder way, and **encourages** her. All in casual speech (반말).

## B. Conversation

**수민:** 오빠, 나 다음 주부터 새 회사로 출근하잖아. 근데 좀 떨려.
> Hey, I start at the new company next week, you know? But I'm kind of nervous.

**지훈:** 아 맞다, 다음 주였지. 떨리는 거 완전 이해돼. 새로운 데 가면 원래 다 그렇잖아.
> Oh right, it was next week. I totally get feeling nervous. Anyone feels that way going somewhere new.

**수민:** 사람들도 다 처음 보는 사람이고, 괜히 실수할까 봐 걱정돼.
> Everyone there is a stranger too, and I'm worried I'll end up making mistakes for no reason.

**지훈:** 근데 그거 오히려 잘하고 싶어서 그런 거잖아. 관심 없으면 긴장도 안 하지.
> But that's actually because you want to do well, isn't it? If you didn't care, you wouldn't even be nervous.

**수민:** 그렇게 들으니까 좀 낫다. 그래도 첫날은 진짜 긴장될 것 같아.
> Hearing it that way makes me feel a little better. Still, the first day's going to be really nerve-wracking.

**지훈:** 응, 처음이 제일 떨리는 것 같아. 근데 너 원래 사람들이랑 금방 친해지잖아. 며칠만 지나면 훨씬 편해질 거야.
> Yeah, the first time's always the most nerve-wracking. But you always get close to people quickly, remember? After just a few days it'll feel much more comfortable.

**수민:** 그러면 좋겠다. 빨리 적응했으면 좋겠어.
> I hope so. I hope I adjust quickly.

**지훈:** 잘할 거야. 하다 보면 어느새 익숙해져 있을 거야. 그러니까 너무 걱정 안 해도 될 것 같아.
> You'll do great. Once you get into it, you'll be used to it before you know it. So I don't think you need to worry too much.

## D. Language You Need

### -잖아 — "you know / you always..., remember" (reassuring)

**Meaning:** Point to something you both already know is true or normal, to gently
reassure the other person. "원래 다 그렇잖아" = that's just how it always is.
"너 원래 ~잖아" = you always do ~, remember?

**Used here:**

> 새로운 데 가면 원래 다 그렇잖아.
> 너 원래 사람들이랑 금방 친해지잖아.

**What changes:**
- Attach -잖아 to the plain form: 그렇다 → 그렇잖아, 친해지다 → 친해지잖아.

[[respond]]
Q: Reassure her with -잖아: "You always do well, remember." (casual)
A: 너 원래 잘하잖아.
[[/respond]]

### 하다 보면 — "once you get into it / as you keep doing it, ..."

**Meaning:** Something changes on its own as you keep doing it. A gentle way to
encourage — you point at how things naturally get easier, without giving an order.

**Basic shape:**

> verb stem + -다 보면 + (result)

**Used here:**

> 하다 보면 어느새 익숙해져 있을 거야.

[[respond]]
Q: Encourage her: "Once you keep doing it, it'll get easier."
A: 하다 보면 쉬워질 거야.
[[/respond]]

### -ㄹ 거야 / -ㄹ 것 같아 — reassure softly (instead of commanding)

**Meaning:** Rather than telling her what to do ("do this," "don't worry"), say
what you *think* will happen. It sounds warm and hopeful, not bossy.

**Used here:**

> 며칠만 지나면 훨씬 편해질 거야.
> 너무 걱정 안 해도 될 것 같아.

**What changes:**
- 편해지다 → 편해질 거야 (it'll get comfortable)
- 괜찮다 → 괜찮을 것 같아 (I think it'll be okay)

[[respond]]
Q: Reassure her softly: "I think it'll all be okay."
A:
- 다 괜찮을 거야.
- 다 잘될 것 같아.
[[/respond]]

## G. Transfer

Same warm, reassuring language — new situation: this time she's nervous about a
presentation tomorrow, not a new job.

[[respond]]
Q: She says "내일 발표인데 실수할까 봐 걱정돼." Reframe it kindly: "That just means you prepared hard for it." (use -잖아)
A: 그만큼 열심히 준비했다는 거잖아.
[[/respond]]

[[respond]]
Q: Encourage her about the presentation: "Once you start, it'll be fine." (use 하다 보면 or 시작하면)
A:
- 일단 시작하면 괜찮을 거야.
- 하다 보면 금방 끝날 거야.
[[/respond]]
